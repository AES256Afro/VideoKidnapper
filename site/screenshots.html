A visual tour

Every screenshot is the real app, straight from the window.

The whole flow in ten seconds

Load a clip, drag the trim handles, drop a caption on it, export a GIF. That's the core loop VideoKidnapper is built around.

Edit: everything on one screen

Three steps across the top: Import, Edit, Export. Edit fits without scrolling: media on the left, the preview in the middle, an Inspector on the right that edits one selected thing at a time, and a track timeline underneath with ranges, captions, overlays, a filmstrip and the waveform.

Export: every decision on one page

What to export (the selection plus saved ranges, joined or separate), what it is made for, the format and quality, where it goes and what it is called. The summary says exactly what you will get before you click.

Captions in the Inspector

Pick a caption on the timeline and the Text tab styles it: preset looks, font, color, outline, box, position and timing. Drag its clip to move it in time, or drag an edge to retime it.

Captions that follow the action

Pin a caption to a moving subject and it tracks them across the frame, like the “click for more” memes. Here one caption follows the shape through three moments of the same clip. Drag to set the path by hand, or click ⚡ Auto-track and let OpenCV follow the subject for you. The preview matches the export the whole way.

Import from a link, or a whole batch

Paste a link (or Ctrl+V from anywhere) and the platform lights up: YouTube, Instagram, X, Reddit, Bluesky, Facebook, and 1,000+ more. Browser-cookie support handles private videos; the batch queue grabs a list in order and drops any one straight into the editor.

Start here: every way to load a video

Import lays it out: paste a link, open a file, record your screen, or pick up a recent project. You can also drop a file on the Edit preview. Captions come in from an .srt, or from speech with Whisper.

History: your recent exports

Grouped by day with a thumbnail of each, plus format, quality, time and size. Play it or show it in its folder in one click; files that were moved or deleted are greyed out.

A clear first run

A concise welcome explains the workflow and starts with a local video or web link. Editing remains private on your computer.

Projects that survive an interrupted session

Save the complete editing state in a .vidkid file, reopen recent work, or recover the latest atomic autosave after an unexpected close.

Updates that match the install

The app knows how it was installed. Store copies update through the Store; Setup, winget and portable installs open the verified GitHub release; pip and APT use their package manager.

Setup explains every prerequisite

Nothing downloads until you approve. Setup shows what is needed, where it comes from, and live progress. FFmpeg downloads are checked against the publisher's SHA-256 digest before installation.
